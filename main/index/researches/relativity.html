Introduction to Einstein's Theory of Relativity

Albert Einstein's Theory of Relativity consists of two parts: Special Relativity (1905) and General Relativity (1915). Special relativity focuses on objects moving at constant speeds, while general relativity deals with gravitation and the curvature of spacetime.

Special Theory of Relativity

  • Time and space are relative and depend on the observer’s motion.
  • The speed of light is constant in all inertial frames of reference.
  • Mass and energy are related by the famous equation: E = mc²

General Theory of Relativity

General relativity explains gravity not as a force, but as a curvature of spacetime caused by mass. Massive objects like the Sun cause spacetime to curve, and planets orbit the Sun by following the curves in space.

Impact on Science

Einstein's theories revolutionized our understanding of space, time, and gravity. His work predicted the bending of light by gravity, the existence of black holes, and the expansion of the universe.
