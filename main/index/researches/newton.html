Introduction to Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Isaac Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation, formulated in 1687, posited that every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

Key Formula

The formula for Newton’s law of gravitation is:

F = G * (m1 * m2) / r²
  • F is the gravitational force between two masses.
  • m1 and m2 are the two masses involved.
  • r is the distance between the centers of the two masses.
  • G is the gravitational constant (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²).

Impact on Science

Newton’s law unified the motion of objects on Earth and in space, laying the foundation for classical mechanics. It allowed scientists to explain the orbits of planets and moons and paved the way for later developments in physics, including Einstein’s general theory of relativity.
