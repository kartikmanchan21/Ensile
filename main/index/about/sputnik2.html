Sputnik 2 - First Living Creature in Space

Background

Launched on November 3, 1957, Sputnik 2 carried the first living creature, a dog named Laika, into orbit.

Key Achievements

Laika's journey provided valuable data on the biological effects of space travel on living organisms.

Sputnik 2's mission demonstrated the Soviet Union's space capabilities and intensified the space race with the U.S.

Scientific Contributions

Despite Laika's tragic death in space, the mission led to further research on animal spaceflight.

Summary

Sputnik 2's mission, although controversial, represented a significant step in understanding the effects of space travel on living creatures, paving the way for human space exploration.
