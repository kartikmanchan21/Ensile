Chandrayaan-2 Mission Report

India's Second Lunar Mission by ISRO

1. Mission Overview

Chandrayaan-2 is India's second lunar exploration mission, launched on July 22, 2019. The primary goal was to explore the Moon’s south pole region, which is believed to have significant amounts of water ice.

Launch Date: July 22, 2019

Launch Vehicle: GSLV Mk III

Mission Duration: Planned for 1 year (Orbiter continues to operate)

End Date: September 6, 2019 (Lander Vikram lost contact)

2. Mission Objectives

The primary objectives of the Chandrayaan-2 mission were to explore the Moon’s South Pole region and search for water ice, among other scientific goals:

  • Explore the Moon’s south pole region.
  • Search for water ice and study the lunar surface.
  • Study the mineral composition of the Moon using the orbiter and lander.
  • Test new technologies for future space exploration missions.

3. Key Scientific Findings

Although the Vikram lander lost contact during its descent, the orbiter has continued to provide valuable data, contributing to lunar science:

  • Water Ice: Evidence of water ice and hydroxyl on the Moon’s surface, particularly in the polar regions.
  • Lunar Surface Composition: The orbiter continues to send data on the surface’s mineral composition.
  • Lunar Exosphere: The orbiter's observations have provided insights into the Moon's thin atmosphere.

4. Mission Timeline

  • July 22, 2019: Chandrayaan-2 launched from Sriharikota (GSLV Mk III).
  • August 20, 2019: The orbiter successfully entered lunar orbit.
  • September 6, 2019: The Vikram lander lost contact just before landing on the lunar surface.
  • Ongoing: The orbiter continues to operate and send valuable data to Earth.

5. Challenges and Conclusion

The mission faced significant challenges, but it remains a significant achievement for ISRO:

  • Loss of Vikram Lander: The Vikram lander lost contact just before the soft landing attempt.
  • Communication Challenges: The lander's communication failure was a major setback, but the orbiter’s success was still a huge milestone.

Despite these setbacks, Chandrayaan-2 is regarded as a significant success for India’s space program and provides a solid foundation for future lunar missions.

6. Legacy and Impact

Chandrayaan-2’s success in placing the orbiter in lunar orbit and continuing operations has paved the way for future missions, including Chandrayaan-3 and potential manned lunar missions.

The Chandrayaan-2 mission has contributed significantly to lunar exploration. The orbiter’s continued success and the lessons learned from the Vikram lander have made ISRO's future lunar missions stronger and more advanced.
