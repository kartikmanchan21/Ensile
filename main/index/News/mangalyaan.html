Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission) Report

India's First Mars Mission by ISRO

1. Mission Overview

Mangalyaan, officially the Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), was India’s first interplanetary mission, launched on November 5, 2013. It was designed to study the Martian atmosphere, surface, and mineral composition.

Launch Date: November 5, 2013

Launch Vehicle: PSLV-C25

Mission Duration: 6 months (Extended to ongoing)

End Date: Ongoing, as of 2024

2. Mission Objectives

The Mangalyaan mission was designed to:

  • Study the Martian surface and mineral composition.
  • Understand the Martian atmosphere and weather patterns.
  • Examine methane levels in the Martian atmosphere.
  • Test technology for future interplanetary exploration.

3. Key Scientific Findings

The mission has yielded key findings about the Martian surface and atmosphere:

  • Methane Detection: The orbiter detected trace amounts of methane in the Martian atmosphere.
  • Lunar Surface Mapping: Mangalyaan provided high-resolution images of the Martian surface.
  • Dust Storm Patterns: The mission observed seasonal changes in Martian dust storms.

4. Mission Timeline

  • November 5, 2013: Launched from Sriharikota.
  • September 24, 2014: Successfully entered Mars orbit.
  • Ongoing: Continues to send data to Earth and remains operational as of 2024.

5. Challenges and Conclusion

While the mission was a remarkable success, there were challenges:

  • Initial Budget Constraints: The mission was cost-effective, making it a remarkable achievement for India.
  • Limited Communication: Communication with Mars is challenging due to the vast distance.

Despite these challenges, the mission remains a significant milestone in India’s space exploration efforts.

6. Legacy and Impact

India’s successful Mars mission showcased its technological expertise and proved that even with limited resources, great achievements are possible. The mission has laid the foundation for future interplanetary exploration by ISRO.

The Mars Orbiter Mission has inspired numerous space agencies around the world and will continue to influence Mars research for years to come.
